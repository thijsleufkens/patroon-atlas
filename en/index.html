Pattern Atlas

What a dashboard doesn't tell you, and how to see it anyway.

Four scatterplots about a fictional machine builder in Helmond. Each plot asks a question that rarely shows up in standard operational reporting. Meant for manufacturers who have their operational dashboards in order, who notice the same charts no longer surface new insight but suspect there is a pattern running across projects.

The demo

Korver Machinebouw is a fictional special-machine builder in Helmond, three generations, 85 employees. It builds packaging machines for the Dutch food industry: sauces, meat, dairy, canned goods, bakery. The atlas uses two years of invented project data: thirteen customers across five sub-sectors, eight product lines, twenty-seven projects.

Plot 1

Which customers earn, and which cost you money?

Revenue and margin per customer, aggregated across all projects. Color per sub-sector so segment patterns become visible.

Plot 2

Which product lines pay off, and which are moving the wrong way?

An arrow per machine type from 2024 to 2025 in margin × engineering overrun. Not just where each product line sits, but which direction it is moving.

Plot 3

How does the customer-machine portfolio sit?

Customer times machine type as a bubble matrix, with sub-sector grouping. Concentration and empty cells at a glance.

Plot 4

Which customers are different from last year?

An arrow per customer between 2024 and 2025. Shows the shift in revenue and margin at once, not as two separate reports.

What's next

Whether the same patterns show up in your own project records depends on what your records hold: hours per phase, margin per project, customer-product links across multiple years. Not every plot needs all of it.

What does and does not line up in your data, and which patterns matter to you, is worth an honest conversation. There is a free intake at Datawijs met Thijs for that.
