A dashboard shows where you stand. A pattern shows where you are heading.

Operational dashboards answer questions you already have: revenue this month, inventory now, lead time last week. Patterns answer the question behind those: where are you coming from, and where are you heading? Four scatterplots about a fictional machine builder.

Two dimensions at once

What a revenue report doesn't show.

Revenue × margin, customer × product line. Cross-dimensionally, groups become visible that get lost line by line in a table.

Plot 1 — Customers

Which customers earn, and which cost you money?

The pattern: one customer below the margin median is a customer conversation. A whole sub-sector below it, like both Protein customers here, is something else.

Plot 3 — Spread

Which product lines hang on a single customer, and which customers buy more than one machine?

The pattern: concentration risk at a glance. Product lines that hang on one customer, sub-sectors that lean on the same few machine types, and the empty cells that tell you where you are not.

Two years at once

Not a status, a direction.

2024 and 2025 in the same plot, with an arrow between them. The difference between "we are here" and "we are moving here" changes which decision a leadership team makes.

Plot 2 — Product lines

Which product lines pay off, and which are moving the wrong way?

The pattern: one thin-margin Sleeve project is an incident. Three Sleeve projects with an arrow toward the bottom left over two years is a product-line question, not a project question.

Plot 4 — Migration

Which customers are different from last year?

The pattern: not who is big, not who grows. Who shifts, and which way. Growth without margin loss is a different conversation than growth with margin loss.
