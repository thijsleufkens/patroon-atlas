Plot 1 — Customers

Which customers earn, and which cost you money?

A revenue report per customer shows who is big. A margin report per customer shows who is profitable. By bringing both into view at once, and coloring by sub-sector, it becomes visible that margin pressure often does not belong to one customer but to a whole group of customers in the same food category.

One point is one customer, aggregated across all projects in 2024 and 2025. Color follows sub-sector (five categories in the food industry). Bubble size = number of projects. Dashed lines are the median across customers as a reading aid, not a rule.

With your own data: customers from your CRM linked to projects from your project records (Exact, AFAS, Ridder, Isah, Plan-de-CAMpagne). Aggregated revenue and revenue-weighted margin per customer. Sub-sector as an attribute on the customer record, or as a grouping on industry code. Customers become a handful of points, sub-sector a color, and the quadrant makes it immediately clear which segments sit structurally below the median.

How to read this
  • Top right: large customers with good margin. Understanding why this works is worth more than optimizing it. Brouwer Sauzen and Akkermans Suikerwerk sit here.
  • Bottom right: large customers with thin margin. This is where the risk sits that leadership structurally underestimates, because the revenue masks the margin pressure. Vink Diepvries (Dairy) is here now.
  • Top left: small customers with good margin. Often skipped, sometimes unfairly. Klaver Kruiden (Specialty) is the clearest example here.
  • Bottom left: small and thin on margin. Candidate for "why are we still doing this?". Verlinden Vis sits here.
  • Readable by color: both Protein customers (De Vegter, Verlinden) sit below the margin median. That is a sub-sector pattern, not an individual customer question.

A customer as a point is an aggregate across projects. The profit and loss at one customer can cancel each other out, and then the customer ends up in an average spot while under the hood there are four projects with four different stories. The table beside it shows how many projects a customer has.

How to bring this to leadership

A leadership conversation about customer profitability is usually one customer at a time. What this plot adds is that sub-sector can be a pattern. Both Protein customers sit below the median, and that is no coincidence if the Sleeve machine and Stretch wrapper are both troubled product lines (which Protein customers buy). The real conversation is then not about De Vegter or Verlinden separately, but about whether Korver should keep running the Protein-segment machines in their current setup.

Vink Diepvries (Dairy) deserves attention in this conversation because it is a top-3 customer that slid into low-margin territory in 2025. Whether or not to take customer action is the same sub-question as in Migration: what is the path back to the higher margin Vink still had in 2024.
