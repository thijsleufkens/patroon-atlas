Plot 4 — Migration

Which customers are different from last year?

The same customers in 2024 and 2025, in the same scatterplot. One small arrow per customer between the two years. Customers who carry the arrow horizontally or vertically are stable; the diagonal arrows tell the story that is normally hidden behind an annual figure.

Each arrow is one customer. Open circle = 2024, filled circle = 2025. Color follows the direction of the margin change: green up, red down, gray if the margin barely moves. Loose open circles (dotted) are customers who did not return in 2025; filled circles with an amber border are new customers in 2025. Revenue and margin are aggregated at customer level (revenue-weighted margin where there are multiple projects).

With your own data: project data over two or more years from your project records (Exact, AFAS, Ridder, Isah, Plan-de-CAMpagne), grouped by customer number. Every customer who appears in both years becomes an arrow, every new or dropped customer a loose marker. The leadership conversation "who is different from last year?" becomes a picture instead of two annual summaries with absolute differences.

How to read this
  • Short arrow: stable customer. Same revenue, same margin, same spot in the quadrant. No story to tell, and that in itself is an outcome.
  • Horizontal to the right: customer grows in revenue without losing margin. Often a repeat order or follow-up machine, worth a conversation about why this works.
  • Diagonal downward: revenue rises, margin drops. Usually a larger project where the pricing does not scale with the complexity. You do not see this in an annual report where revenue comes first.
  • Straight down: comparable order but thinner. This is usually where the real question sits: is this a one-off or structural?

Quadrant migration over two years is hard to replicate in standard dashboards. That is exactly what makes it useful here: the plot shows in one picture what is otherwise scattered across a year-over-year table, a margin report, and a customer-revenue overview.

How to bring this to leadership

A year-over-year report usually speaks in volume (revenue grows or shrinks) or margin (the average goes up or down). What this plot adds is the direction per customer: growth without margin loss is a different conversation than growth with margin loss.

Vink Diepvries is the pattern that stands out most here: large in revenue but arrow steeply down in margin, with a shift to Packaging line as the cause. The recommendation that can roll out of this is not "bring customer Vink back to the old margin level" (often unachievable with a product-line shift) but "what is an acceptable margin level on Packaging line and can we calibrate the quote to it".

The still arrows (short arrow, little movement) are usually not the trigger for a leadership conversation. But together they are the signal that the stable base is still there, which tempers how much panic the diagonal arrows deserve.
