Plot 2 — Product lines

Which product lines pay off, and which are moving the wrong way?

Margin per project is in order for most manufacturers, but the pattern across a product line only emerges once you aggregate across multiple projects. One thin-margin Sleeve machine project is an incident. Three thin-margin Sleeve machines with an arrow pointing down over two years is something else. The difference between "S22 sits top left" and "S22 is moving further top left" changes which decision a leadership team makes.

One arrow per machine type, from the 2024 position to the 2025 position in revenue × margin space. Open marker = 2024, filled marker = 2025. Color of bubble and arrow follows the engineering overrun in 2025: green under 15%, amber between 15 and 50%, red above 50%. Packaging line L100 is new in 2025 and has no arrow, just a marker with an amber border. Dashed lines are medians across the 2025 endpoints.

With your own data: project data from your project records (Exact, AFAS, Ridder, Isah, Plan-de-CAMpagne) grouped by product line (machine type, service category, or whatever group you steer on internally) and by year. Revenue-weighted margin and weighted engineering overrun per year. The arrow between two years shows where each product line is heading; the color warns on the engineering side. Both sit implicitly in margin-per-year reports.

How to read this
  • Arrow toward top right: product line grows in revenue and gains on margin. Labeling machine E12 does that: revenue nearly doubled, margin stable.
  • Arrow toward bottom left: shrinking and margin drop. Sleeve machine S22 is the sharpest example here, on top of a red color because the engineering overrun sits well above half.
  • Short arrow top right or bottom right: workhorse. Tray loader T15 stable top right, green because overrun is minimal.
  • Red bubble bottom left with a short arrow: small, thin-margin product line with extreme engineering overrun. Stretch wrapper W8 falls through zero toward negative on this side. Even without a big arrow, that is the leadership topic.
  • Marker with an amber border and no arrow: product line new in 2025. Packaging line L100 sits large and with a healthy margin, but the amber border says there is no comparison point yet and the bubble color (amber) says the engineering overrun is already elevated.

At customer level this plot explains the sub-sector pattern: the Protein customers hang on Sleeve and Stretch wrapper, and those are the two product lines that turn red and move the wrong way on margin. The customers are red because their product line is red and getting redder.

How to bring this to leadership

A margin question at product-line level is a different question than at project level. Project level usually ends in "we will do it differently next time". Product-line level ends in a choice: keep going under existing terms, raise the estimate, raise the price, or stop. The arrow adds one more dimension, direction, and that is the difference between "we monitor" and "we decide".

Concretely for this data: Sleeve machine S22 and Stretch wrapper W8 both sit bottom left, both red from extreme engineering overrun. The Sleeve arrow points bottom left: smaller in revenue, lower in margin than in 2024. Stretch wrapper has a short arrow but falls through zero toward negative on the margin axis. The recommendation that can roll out of this plot is not "do Sleeve better next time" but "only quote Sleeve for 2026 with a type-specific hours surcharge, and pause Stretch wrapper until we understand the engineering underestimation on that product line". The choice is grounded in two years of pattern, not one year's outcome.

Packaging line L100 is interesting in another way: new in 2025 but already large in revenue, with a healthy 15% margin weighted across two orders. That is positive, but on one order (Vink Diepvries) the margin was only 7%. Recommendation: set the estimate tighter for the third and fourth L100 quote than at Vink, otherwise the weighted margin shifts down in 2026 and this product line tips the same way.

Tray loader T15 and Filling station M60 sit top right with short arrows, both green from low overrun. There is nothing to change there, and a leadership decision on the bad product lines should not come at the expense of engineering capacity for these workhorses. Filling station M40 does have a big arrow to the left (Bremer Frisdrank bought in 2024 but not in 2025), but the margin went up: that is a mix effect, not a product-line question.
